      How does it actually work?
      1. It reads. It goes through a list of about 32,000 real first names.
      2. It notices pairs. For every letter, it keeps track of which letter came right after it, and how often. After q it nearly always sees u. Names often end after a or n.
      3. It makes names. To make a new name, it picks a first letter the way real names do, then keeps picking each next letter based only on the one before it, until it decides to stop.
      4. That's the whole trick. It has no idea what came two letters back. That's why you get things like staiyaubrtthrigotai. Big AI models work on the same idea, only they look much further back and have learned from far more text.

      By counting means the machine literally tallied the pairs. By practice means it started with random guesses and slowly corrected itself, a thousand times over, which is how bigger AI models learn. Both end up with almost exactly the same memory. Flip the switch at the top and compare.
